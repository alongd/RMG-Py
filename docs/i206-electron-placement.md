# I-206 — declare electron placement for the two carried plasma families

## What changed

Two entries added to `FAMILY_ELECTRON_PLACEMENT` in `rmgpy/electron_placement.py`:

```python
'Plasma_Electron_Impact_Ionization': (1, 2),
'Plasma_Radiative_Recombination': (1, 0),
```

Without them, each family raised `ElectronPlacementError` naming itself the moment the resolver was
asked to place its electron — the module's designed refusal (placement is family-declared, never
inferred from the net scalar), not a bug. Both families are carried on the unmerged RMG-database
branch `i205-dataless`.

### 2. Each pair derived independently from the family's template + `electrons`

From `RMG-database-i205-dataless/input/kinetics/families/*/groups.py`:

| Family | template | `electrons` | incident (reactant_count) | out (product_count) | net = p − r | pair |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Plasma_Electron_Impact_Ionization` | `A_rad -> A+` | `+1` | 1 (one e⁻ in) | 2 (two e⁻ out) | `2 − 1 = +1` ✓ | `(1, 2)` |
| `Plasma_Radiative_Recombination` | `A+ + e⁻ -> A + hv` | `−1` | 1 (one e⁻ captured) | 0 (none out) | `0 − 1 = −1` ✓ | `(1, 0)` |

I agree with both numbers in the ticket table. Each is identical to its sibling kinetics-library's
existing pair (`PlasmaElectronImpactIonization: (1, 2)`, `PlasmaRadiativeRecombination: (1, 0)`), a
useful check — but the two new keys are the **family** labels (underscored), distinct from the
library labels. The map is keyed on `Reaction.family` (`get_placement_owner` reads
`reaction.family` first); a reaction generated by `Plasma_Electron_Impact_Ionization` carries that
family label, not the library's, so the family form needs its own key and does not shadow the
library's.

### 3. The map's rule and its validation

The module docstring: the two numbers are `(reactant_count, product_count)`; `reactant_count` is the
**incident order** (what the rate coefficient's dimensionality must match), and
`product_count − reactant_count` is the **net change**, the only quantity `Reaction.electrons` is
compared against. The resolver enforces `net_electrons == product_count − reactant_count` (step 5).
Both new pairs satisfy it against their family's `electrons`: `2 − 1 = +1` and `0 − 1 = −1`.

### The resulting reaction is right, not merely produced

- **Electron bookkeeping.** Ionization view `[Li] + e- => [Li+] + e- + e-` places (1, 2) and
  balances in the `E` pseudo-element (1 = 1). Radiative view `[Li+] + e- => [Li]` places (1, 0) and
  balances (0 = 0). `check_electron_balance` passes for both — the structural proof (step 10) that
  the electron went to the correct sides.
- **Rate order.** Each view has two reactants (heavy species + the incident electron). Both rate
  coefficients are `m^3/(mol*s)` = order 2, consistent with the two-reactant view. (The rate rules
  materialise as `ArrheniusEP`, which `get_plasma_rate_order` does not recognise, so the step-11
  cross-check records `RATE_ORDER_UNRESOLVABLE` — its own outcome, correctly not read as agreement —
  rather than firing. The units→order check above is the independent confirmation.)

## Findings for whoever sequences the merges

1. **Ordering (stated by the ticket, restated here).** The two families live on the unmerged
   RMG-database branch `i205-dataless`. This RMG-Py change is useless alone and must land **before or
   together with** that branch; landing that branch without this one leaves two families that raise
   on first use.

2. **A recorded decision is reversed — flagged, not slipped in.**
   `i113…::test_no_electron_impact_ionisation_family_is_shipped` was a deliberate tripwire asserting
   no key starting with `Plasma_Electron_Impact_Ionization` exists, encoding the I-116/I-154 ruling
   that electron-impact ionisation is owned by a kinetics **library** and the **family** was
   declined "to avoid a second, disagreeing owner for the same chemistry." The `i205-dataless`
   branch reverses that by carrying the family (a data-less Arrhenius estimate, distinct from the
   library's Voronov fit), and this ticket declares its placement. I renamed the test to
   `test_electron_impact_ionisation_family_is_now_shipped` and inverted its assertion, documenting
   the reversal in-place. The two owners declare the **same** pair `(1, 2)` — no placement
   disagreement.

3. **The declaration PREVENTS a duplicate, and the specific pair `(1, 2)` is what makes the sourced
   value win.** The dual-owner path was measured against the code (by the `i205-dataless` worker and
   re-checked here); the mechanism is real but its valence is the opposite of a hazard. This entry is
   load-bearing twice: it lets the family run at all, and `(1, 2)` is what resolves the lithium
   collision in favour of the sourced datum.

   The identity predicate, `rmgpy/rmg/model.py:2356` `are_identical_species_references`
   (body 2378-2391), compares the reactant/product **references**, `specific_collider`, and
   `get_electron_placement_counts(...)` per side — and **nothing else**: no owner, no kinetics, no
   provenance. Work the two cases for the one species where the library and the family overlap:

   - **WITHOUT this declaration.** The family reaction falls back to the net rule and gets `(0, 1)`;
     the library reaction has `(1, 2)`. They do **not** compare identical, so RMG keeps **both** — a
     genuine duplicate for the same chemistry with conflicting rates, one sourced (Voronov) and one
     an order-of-magnitude estimate. (And the family reaction separately raises
     `ElectronPlacementError` at the reactor, since it has no declaration.)
   - **WITH this declaration.** Both are `(1, 2)`, they compare identical, and
     `check_for_existing_reaction` (`model.py:481`, and `:523` cross-library) returns the
     **incumbent**; `make_new_reaction` (`model.py:600-602`) does `if found: return rxn, False`,
     dropping the newcomer with no rate comparison. The incumbent is **always the sourced Voronov
     library reaction**, because reaction libraries are pre-loaded to the edge —
     `rmgpy/rmg/main.py:795-796` `add_reaction_library_to_edge` — strictly before the
     family-enlargement loop (`main.py:869`/`872`, `1016-1037`). So the collapse resolves in favour
     of the sourced value, every time.

   Measured both ways:

   ```
   WITH    (entry present): family (1, 2) == library (1, 2)  -> are_identical = True  (library wins)
   WITHOUT (entry removed):  family (0, 1) != library (1, 2)  -> are_identical = False (both kept)
   ```

   **The overlap is lithium and only lithium.** The `PlasmaElectronImpactIonization` library has
   exactly one entry, `[Li] => [Li+]`. The family also generates `N => N+`, `O => O+`, and so on,
   where the library is silent and the family is the sole source with nothing to displace. The
   family generalizes the chemistry and collides only where the library already speaks.

   **The protection rests on registration ORDER, not a source-priority rule** — the part a future
   reader needs and will not otherwise find. `are_identical_species_references` knows nothing of
   which owner is authoritative; the sourced value wins purely because it is registered first
   (`main.py:795-796` before enlargement). Two named conditions therefore make the *estimate* the
   operative Li rate: **(a)** a deck that loads the family but not the library — no sourced value is
   present to displace; **(b)** a family estimate baked into a seed mechanism, which lands in core at
   `main.py:790-791` (`add_seed_mechanism_to_core`) **ahead** of libraries at `:795-796` —
   pathological and self-inflicted. Neither is reachable by any current RMG-Py test (the family
   exists only in the unmerged `i205-dataless` database); both are recorded here for whoever
   sequences the family carry.
